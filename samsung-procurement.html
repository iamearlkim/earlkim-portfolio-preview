Integrated material purchasing process

Samsung's buyers negotiated prices and distributed volumes in Excel, across seven different material codes and four systems. As part of Samsung's next-generation ERP (N-ERP), we turned that manual work into an automated purchasing process — with end users shaping every phase.

01 — The challenge

Negotiation by spreadsheet

Negotiation preparation and price distribution were done by hand in Excel. Buyers worked with seven different material codes globally and four separate systems to gather data and communicate — a process prone to human error.

Our design direction: improve efficiency, shorten processing and decision time, simplify complex work, make data visible, and minimize manual tasks through process automation.

02 — A user-centered process

From design thinking workshops to final user tests, end users were key stakeholders. We uncovered pain points and hidden problems with them, had them generate the first ideas for the new process, and iterated up to ten times on their feedback.

  1. Discover · W2–W3Design thinking workshop (12 end users, 8 project owners), user interviews (13 end users) and a feedback session (11 end users).
  2. Design · W4–W5Two online usability tests in SAP Build (32 and 23 user responses), a knowledge session, and an offline test and survey.
  3. OutcomesAs-is process and journey maps, stakeholder map, key UX values, then lo-fi prototypes refined into high-fidelity Fiori designs.
03 — From as-is to to-be

Storyboards contrasted the manual process with an automated one where users understand current conditions at a glance.

  1. Negotiation dashboardWas: quarterly Excel data gathering, hard to track afterward. Now: a negotiation-specific dashboard shows current work and progress at a glance.
  2. Automated RFQ and simulationWas: per-vendor price and allocation negotiated by hand, with little history. Now: RPA filters RFQ errors and users simulate prices and allocations.
  3. Automatic reportingWas: repeated report-making before, during and after negotiation. Now: reports are visualized automatically, with views for buyers and part or group leaders.
  4. Standardized workWas: non-standard, person-dependent work. Now: standardized, efficient processes that support strategic decisions.
04 — Rapid prototyping

We sketched the flows by hand and tested them with users before moving to high fidelity.

05 — High-fidelity design

Clickable high-fidelity prototypes in Axure, built on the SAP Fiori design system and refined through online usability tests.

06 — Outcome

A faster, automated purchasing process

  • Total procurement process time reduced by 42.5%.
  • Negotiation, RFQ, simulation and reporting brought into one connected flow.
  • A design validated by end users at every phase, through up to ten iterations.
