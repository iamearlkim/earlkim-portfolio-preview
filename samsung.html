The challenge

One company, many systems, many personas

Samsung's operations run on many SAP and legacy systems, each serving different teams — buyers, ESG managers, plant operators, supply-chain planners and executives.

Across engagements the recurring problem was the same: data lived in disconnected systems and spreadsheets, and the people who needed it worked in very different ways.

Platform UX strategy

Three principles that carried across every project

  1. One design languageEvery module was built on SAP Fiori, so a buyer, an ESG manager and an executive meet the same patterns, components and behaviors — and new modules feel like part of one platform.
  2. Domain experts as co-designersDesign thinking workshops, interviews and usability tests put end users, process owners and specialists (procurement, EHS, supply chain) at the center of every decision.
  3. Reusable patterns across divisionsOverview dashboards, filterable work lists and calculate-then-review flows repeat across procurement, ESG and planning — designed once, adapted per domain.

What I learned

Designing a platform, not just projects

  • Consistency compounds: each project was faster to design and easier to adopt because it reused patterns users already knew.
  • The deepest insights came from end users and domain experts in the room — workshops and usability tests changed the design more than any assumption.
  • Enterprise UX is as much about how data moves between systems and roles as about the screens on top.
