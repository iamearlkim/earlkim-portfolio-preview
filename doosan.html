Optimizing talent acquisition with ML

Doosan was losing experienced employees and overspending on external hiring. Built on SAP BTP and integrated with SAP SuccessFactors, this platform uses machine learning to boost internal mobility, streamline recruiter screening and support self-directed careers for millennial and Gen Z employees.

01 — The challenge

A talent retention problem

92% of employees who left did so for career development, and 37% left within three years of an internal transfer.

  • High talent turnover — experienced employees were leaving.
  • Rising recruitment costs — heavy spending on external hiring to fill gaps.
  • Hiring bottlenecks — external candidates were harder and more competitive to find.
02 — Exploring

Design thinking workshops as a kickoff

I brought all stakeholders together to align on the as-is internal recruitment process, conceptualize the to-be screen flows, and draw up action plans — producing a use-case diagram, key screen flows and an action plan.

Empathizing with users

6 interviews and thematic analysis

  1. RecruitersOverwhelmed by manual screening and reporting; struggling to balance quality with limited time.
  2. EmployeesSeeking career growth, clear role details and safe, anonymous communication channels.
03 — Design goals

A healthy, sustainable internal hiring culture through technology

  1. Streamlined processReplace Excel-based screening and reporting with a system that cuts recruiters' manual work.
  2. Enhanced job visibilityHelp employees find opportunities and detailed job information easily.
  3. Ensured data protectionPrevent exposure of application information that created workplace friction.
04 — Building the ML foundation

Designing with engineers and data scientists

With the dev team, ML engineers, data scientists and the client's IT team, I framed the data questions: what data recruiters already use, where it lives in the HR system and in what format, how much history is realistic, and which data must be visible and explainable in the UI.

Because there was not yet enough labeled data to justify every recommendation, the first model was built on legacy HR records, with an architecture integrating the legacy system, SAP SuccessFactors and SAP Data Intelligence on SAP BTP.

  • Phase 1 — learn from the past: train on years of internal moves, evaluations and project records to learn what a successful internal placement looks like.
  • Phase 2 — learn from the next year: run the model alongside existing HR processes, log every recommendation and capture how recruiters and managers respond.
05 — Design iterations

Sketches validated with users moved from an SAP SuccessFactors-based flow, to customizable applicant data entry, to ML insights that give recruiters a faster, insightful first screen.

06 — Design solution: intelligent screening

An AI-enhanced candidate list built with SAP Fiori 3.0: switch between job requisitions, refine with a filter bar, read color-coded indicators for each criterion, compare in a table, and see each application's suitability at a glance.

07 — Result

Data-driven people management

  • Internal transfers increased 5×.
  • Turnover dropped by 23%, lowering external recruitment costs.
  • Screening productivity increased 2.5×, with data-driven hiring decisions.
  • Satisfaction and engagement with career development rose 26%, through transparent job information and a trusted, confidential process.
What's next

Future direction

  1. Analytical dashboardSpot trends, drill into root causes and get actionable insights, designed with SAP Fiori Horizon.
  2. Scalable AI explainabilityThree levels — descriptive score, explanatory factors, and advanced explainability with suggested next steps.
  3. Predictive workforce planningConnect recruitment with business strategy using LinkedIn data, internal talent pools, demand and budget forecasts.
Scaling

From project to product

The project scaled into an independent service: Peoply, a cloud SaaS solution built on SAP BTP and SAP SuccessFactors.

08 — Takeaways

What I learned about designing AI

  • AI products need end-to-end collaboration with data scientists, ML engineers and domain experts.
  • Human–AI interaction patterns manage the trade-off between control and automation, and explainability reduces over-reliance.
  • AI works best as an assistant, with transparency and the ability to override.
  • Designing the data experience — input, processing and display — matters as much as the UI.
